Mission & Vision

The big idea behind In Service AI.

Every part of this practice exists to close one gap: between what mission-driven leaders carry and what their systems actually support.

Mission.

In Service AI exists to lighten the load carried by the people doing the world's most meaningful work, using human-centered AI to clear the invisible drag between what you do and what the world can see of it.

Vision.

In Service AI will be known as the partner that helped mission-led organizations run with the discipline of a large institution without losing the heart that makes them special, one organization at a time.

The hidden load you've been carrying.

For a typical 25-person team, the gap between mission and infrastructure quietly consumes the capacity your people need to serve well, respond fully, and reach the next person waiting for the work.

I enter the same number into three different systems. I'm never sure which version is the truth.
If our operations manager leaves, our institutional knowledge leaves with her. Every vacation feels like a potential crisis.
I have to reconstruct our story from spreadsheets every time a funder asks for a report. The board sees yesterday's data, not today's.
Two stacked grant deadlines feel like a personal crisis. Sometimes we just don't apply.
Strategy meetings turn into downloads from my head, because there's no shared foundation for the team to see.

None of this is a personal failing. It's what happens when systems get built under pressure instead of by design, and no dashboard or AI tool fixes that on its own.

Here is what that load costs before anyone puts a number on it.

6–8hours per person, per week
~7,800hours a year — 3.75 full-time employees
$318,750conservative lost productivity, annually

And when that strain leads even two mid-level staff members to leave, the cost isn't just the $170,000 in rehiring and retraining. It's the context, trust, and institutional knowledge that leaves with them.

When the lost hours become visible, the path forward becomes less overwhelming. You don't need to replace everything your team has built. You need a foundation that can hold the weight.

Most organizations don't have an AI problem. They have an operational foundation problem. Until your systems, processes, and information work together, every new tool simply adds another layer of complexity.
